I help people publicly — especially when scam patterns look exploitable. I keep evidence chains and prefer local-first tools so advice survives without telemetry or hostile-channel interference.

What I do

01. Public-help analyses

Pattern-based scam detection on social media posts; response templates that hold up to screenshot-takers. Worked "in public" — comments and posts are the artifacts.

Methodology: signal-vs-noise line items, link to source-message screenshot-level detail.

02. Adjudication evidence chains

When someone claims "scam" or "legit", I keep the receipts — URLs, timestamps, message body, who-said-what-when. Append-only logs; nothing silently rewritten.

Tools: append-only JSONL audits, immutable-when-possible storage, deliberate non-overwriting edits.

03. Workflow on shared platforms

Most scams live on Facebook/Messenger. The workflow has to live there too — quick recognition, plain-English response, deal-breakers for legal/audience-blowback risk.

Output: comment reply, post, or thread-safe reply macro; never the broader platform. Triage the posting audience first.

04. Local-first tooling

Where possible, run demos offline so adversarial fingerprints (API-key leaks, telemetry) can't compromise an active investigation. OpenRouter + Ollama + local ComfyUI cover most cases.

Anti-fingerprint: prefer stdlib tooling, no third-party SDK in research dirs, audit before sharing helper scripts.
